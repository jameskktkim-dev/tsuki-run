# Gentle Training Guide

## Purpose

Help runners understand how they might approach a future running goal.

This is not a fixed training schedule.

It is a flexible guide that offers structure without pressure.

---

## User Inputs

### Goal

Required.

Options:

- 5K
- 10K
- Half Marathon
- Marathon
- General Fitness
- Other

### Target Date

Required when the user is preparing for an event.

### Target Time

Optional.

### Recent Running

- Average runs per week
- Total distance in the past 3 months
- Longest run in the past 3 months

### Recent Race Times

Optional.

- 5K
- 10K
- Half Marathon
- Marathon

### Experience Level

- New Runner
- Returning Runner
- Regular Runner
- Experienced Runner

### Preferences

Optional.

- Preferred runs per week
- Preferred long-run day
- Current concerns or limitations

---

## Guide Structure

### 1. Your Starting Point

Briefly reflect the runner's current experience and recent running.

Do not judge whether the runner is fit enough.

---

### 2. Suggested Weekly Rhythm

Suggest a flexible number and mix of weekly runs.

Examples:

- easy runs
- a gradually longer run
- optional faster running
- rest and recovery

Avoid assigning exact weekdays unless the user requests them.

---

### 3. Training Phases

Organize the time before the goal into broad phases.

Example:

- Settling In
- Building Rhythm
- Growing Endurance
- Preparing for the Goal
- Resting Before Race Day

The number and length of phases should depend on the available time.

---

### 4. Phase Guidance

For each phase, provide:

- approximate duration
- suggested weekly rhythm
- general long-run direction
- one main focus
- optional adjustments

Do not create a rigid daily schedule.

---

### 5. Gentle Reminders

Include a few brief reminders about:

- gradual progression
- recovery
- flexibility
- pain and discomfort
- adapting around everyday life

Do not diagnose injuries.

---

### 6. Closing Thought

End with a calm reminder that the guide can change.

Example:

"This guide is a place to begin, not a schedule you need to follow perfectly."

---

## Tone

Clear.

Calm.

Supportive.

Practical, but never commanding.

Avoid excessive poetic language because the runner needs understandable guidance.

---

## Safety Rules

Do not guarantee a target time.

Do not present estimated race times as certain.

Do not recommend training through pain.

Do not provide medical diagnoses.

Do not suggest sudden increases in running volume.

Do not shame the runner for their current fitness.

When the goal appears ambitious, explain this honestly and gently.

---

## Preferred Language

Prefer:

- could
- may
- perhaps
- a possible rhythm
- one way to approach this
- adjust as needed
- when it feels comfortable

Avoid:

- must
- have to
- no excuses
- push through
- guaranteed
- perfect plan
- failure

---

## Final Principle

The guide should reduce uncertainty without removing the runner's freedom.

It should offer direction while leaving room for life.
